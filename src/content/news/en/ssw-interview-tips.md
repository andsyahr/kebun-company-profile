Most SSW hiring interviews are 30-minute online sessions. Fluency is easy to hear — but it is not what predicts whether a worker thrives for years. From our side of hundreds of interviews, here is what does.

## Look for learning velocity, not just current level

Language level at interview time reflects training progress, which varies. Ask *"When did you start studying Japanese?"* and *"How many hours a day do you study?"* — the trajectory tells you more about year two than today's fluency does.

## Three signals worth probing

1. **Specific motivation** — can the candidate explain why this industry and where they want to be in five years?
2. **Family support** — in Indonesia, family opinion strongly affects whether a worker stays. Ask directly.
3. **Behavior when stuck** — "What do you do when you don't understand an instruction?" reveals whether the horenso habit will take root.

## Interviews work better as confirmation, not discovery

We provide each candidate's training records — language progress, attendance, instructor notes — before the interview, so the meeting confirms a good match rather than starting from zero.
