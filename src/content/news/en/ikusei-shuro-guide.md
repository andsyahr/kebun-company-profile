Japan is replacing its Technical Intern Training Program (TITP) with a new system called **Ikusei Shuro** (育成就労, "employment for skill development"). Here is what changes.

## Why the change?

TITP was officially framed as skills transfer for international contribution, while in practice interns worked as regular labor without matching protections. The new system addresses that gap directly.

## Key changes

1. **Honest objectives** — the system openly aims to secure and develop foreign workers, with clearer legal protections.
2. **A career path** — roughly three years of development designed to lead into the Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) status, and onward to the renewable SSW type 2.
3. **Job mobility** — workers may change employers under certain conditions, a near-impossibility under TITP.
4. **Stricter rules for sending organizations** — including fee transparency, raising the bar for who can send workers at all.

## What it means in practice

For employers: retention quality matters more than ever, and choosing a compliant, education-focused sending organization becomes a strategic decision. For workers: Japanese language remains the single biggest lever for better options.
